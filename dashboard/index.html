India's households by income class

Share of households. Destitute under ₹1.25L a year, Aspirers ₹1.25–5L, Middle class ₹5–30L, Rich above ₹30L. 2030-31 and 2046-47 are PRICE projections.

Households that earn it vs individuals who declare it

Households above each income (PRICE, 2020-21) vs individual returns declaring that much gross income for the same year (AY 2021-22). Log scale.

Reading the gap fairly. A household can pool several earners, so two ₹15 lakh salaries make one ₹30 lakh household. Survey income also includes agricultural income, which is tax-exempt and never appears on a return. PRICE adjusts reported incomes toward national-accounts totals. Even so, the gap widens at every step up: about 10× at ₹30 lakh and over 40× at ₹2 crore.

Where city numbers come from. CBDT does not publish return statistics by city. City medians come from PRICE's ICE 360 household survey, which reports household counts by income class for India's ten richest cities. Hyderabad and Gurugram are not on that list, so they appear only in the Hurun counts. Tax collections are published by state and attributed by the taxpayer's communication address, so company head offices inflate Mumbai, Bengaluru and Delhi.

Bengaluru. Its 2020-21 survey median sits below Chennai's and Pune's. That was the first pandemic year, and Bengaluru's official per-capita income (₹7.8 lakh) is the highest on the list, so treat its survey median with care.

Telangana vs Andhra Pradesh: from FY 2023-24 Telangana's collections jump and Andhra Pradesh's halve. This looks like a re-mapping of addresses after the state split, not a real change in incomes.

METHOD & SOURCES

How these numbers were built

Data. CBDT "Income Tax Return Statistics" for AY 2014-15 through AY 2023-24, the ten latest years published. Each report tabulates individual returns by income slab, with the count, total and average for gross total income, salary, business income, capital gains, returned income and tax payable. Macro series come from CBDT "Time Series Data, FY 2000-01 to 2024-25" (Nov 2025). An assessment year (AY) reports income earned in the previous financial year: AY 2023-24 covers FY 2022-23.

Percentiles. Inside each CBDT slab the shape is fitted with a truncated Pareto curve that reproduces the slab's published count and average exactly. Percentiles, top-income shares and the calculator read from that curve. The top 1% figures are among filers. The "top 1% of all Indians" line assumes everyone who does not file earns less than the filers who do.

Salary vs business. One return can report both. "Salary earners" are returns with salary income above zero, and "business earners" are returns with business income above zero.

External context (not from CBDT, marked where used): World Bank population; PRICE ICE 360 Surveys 2014/2016/2021 ("The Rise of India's Middle Class"), the source of every household and city-median figure; Mercedes-Benz Hurun India Wealth Report 2025 (8.71 lakh households with net worth ≥ ₹8.5 crore); official district per-capita income from the Maharashtra, Delhi, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka statistics departments (Ahmedabad, Surat and Kolkata from secondary or older estimates); PIB's AY 2024-25 form-wise ITR count; luxury car sales in calendar 2024 (~51,200 units, Autocar Pro).

City medians. PRICE gives household counts in eight income classes per city. The median is read off the cumulative count, assuming incomes spread geometrically inside each class.

Limits. These are the incomes people declare, after CBDT's consistency checks (about 0.1% of returns dropped). Agricultural income is exempt and absent. Wealth is not income, so the Hurun comparison is directional only.
